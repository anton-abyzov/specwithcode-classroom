    What are we working on, Maya?

    An assistant, not a shortcut. It won't do your homework for you. It plans with you, gives hints and asks questions, and leaves the next step to you, so you understand it and can explain it in class.

    1. SpecFirst, the goal and a short checklist of what "done" looks like.
    2. CodeThen the work: slides, a worked solution or a real app, built against that checklist.
    3. CheckYou do the "your turn" steps and tick off each item yourself.
    Runs on the OpenAI Agents SDK and the OpenAI API, built with Codex. Made at the DevDay Exchange hackathon in Miami.

    How it works

    1. The school connects what it already has. OpenAI plans with GPT-6 Luna credits, the Gemini free tier, a low-cost DeepSeek backup, and school computers signed in to Codex.
    2. A coach sends each request to a specialist. Slides, math and physics, or app building. Each one starts with a spec: the goal and a checklist the student can verify. That is the SpecWithCode idea: spec first, then the work, then the student checks it. For students it is an assistant, not a shortcut: hints, questions and "your turn" steps, never finished homework.
    3. Every step goes to the first one with tokens left. If it runs out or goes down mid-answer, the next one finishes the job. Students never see an error, families pay nothing, and every call is in the usage log with the provider's receipt.

    Student mode coaches and never hands over finished work. Teacher mode builds class materials (quizzes with answer keys, lesson decks, worked examples, practice apps) and checks student answers with feedback and what to reteach. Every request passes a K-12 safety check.
